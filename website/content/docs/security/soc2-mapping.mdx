The [AICPA SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc-2) (TSC 2017 with 2022 Revised Points of Focus) are the framework an independent CPA firm uses to perform a SOC 2 examination of a service organisation. This page shows how Attestix's hash-chained audit records, server-signed Verifiable Credentials, and provenance records map to the TSC, focused on the technology-heavy criteria (Common Criteria CC1-CC9 and Processing Integrity PI1) where chain-hashed evidence is most useful.

## Why this matters

**SOC 2 is an attestation, not a certification.** This distinction is load-bearing:

- A **certification** (ISO 27001, ISO 42001) is a binary YES/NO awarded by an accredited certification body. The organisation displays a cert mark.
- An **attestation** (SOC 2) is a CPA firm's professional opinion on whether the service organisation's controls were suitably designed (Type I) or designed *and* operating effectively (Type II) to meet the relevant Trust Services Criteria over a stated audit period. The deliverable is a SOC 2 report (~80 to 200 pages) with a one-page CPA opinion letter on top.

**Attestix does not ship SOC 2 attestations and is not a CPA firm.** We do not issue an opinion. What Attestix ships is **the evidence plumbing your CPA's auditor can use** during your own SOC 2 examination, particularly for the technology-heavy criteria where tamper-evident event logs and server-signed records can reduce auditor evidence-gathering.

## The honest coverage table

The TSC 2017 contains roughly 60 criteria across the five categories (the exact count depends on how the Privacy criteria are grouped; check the current AICPA publication for your examination). The table below covers 30 representative rows focused on Common Criteria (CC1-CC9, the Security category required for every SOC 2 report) and Processing Integrity (PI1).

Items marked "planned" are not in Attestix 0.4.1. Named credential types such as `ProviderAssertionCredential`, `SecurityCheckCredential`, `IncidentReportCredential`, and `VerifiableCheckResult` are planned; today you can issue a credential of any custom type with `issue_credential`. All signatures are made by the Attestix server key, not by per-actor keys, and structured audit events are hash-chained but not signed.

### Tally (the 30 rows above)

- **evidence-substrate** (Attestix produces tamper-evident or server-signed records your auditor can examine): 9

**Where it is most useful for SOC 2:** CC6 (a record of identities and delegated capabilities, not enforcement), CC8 (change records for Attestix-managed data), and PI1.3 (tamper-evident processing records).

> **Important caveat: `security_check_id` is planned for v0.5.0.** As of Attestix 0.4.1 the underlying events are emitted today, but they are NOT tagged with a `soc2.*` discriminator.

## What we don't do

- **We do not issue SOC 2 reports.** The CPA firm performing your examination is the only entity that issues SOC 2 reports.
- **We do not author your policies.** Policy authorship lives in your DMS, with Vanta / Drata / Secureframe templates, or with internal counsel.
- **We do not shepherd your SOC 2 readiness end-to-end.** That is what compliance platforms (Vanta, Drata, Secureframe, Strike Graph, AuditBoard) do: auditor coordination, employee training, evidence collection workflows, continuous monitoring dashboards. They sit *above* the evidence layer; we sit *below*. The two stack.
- **We do not do physical security (CC6.4-CC6.5).** Your data-centre operator's SOC 2 carve-out covers that.
- **We are not your IAM enforcement layer.** We record who attempted what; your Okta / Auth0 / Entra IAM enforces access policies.
- **We are not your SIEM.** We produce tamper-evident forensic records; your Splunk / Datadog / Honeycomb ingests + dashboards.

## How to verify our coverage yourself

### Python / CLI

```bash
# Show an agent's log_action trail and re-check its hash chain
attestix audit <agent-id>

# Export a portability bundle for your auditor (SHA-256 manifest included)
attestix export my-soc2-evidence.tar.gz

# Check a bundle's manifest, per-table hashes, and audit chain without importing
attestix import my-soc2-evidence.tar.gz --verify-only
```

To verify the structured audit-event chain in Python (there is no `verify-chain` CLI command):

```python
from attestix.audit import AUDIT_COLLECTION, verify_chain
from attestix.storage import default_repository

events = default_repository().list(AUDIT_COLLECTION, id_field="event_id")
print(verify_chain(events).valid)
```

### JavaScript / browser

```bash
npm install attestix
```

```javascript
import { verifyCredential } from "attestix";

const result = verifyCredential(credentialJson);
// Checks the Ed25519 signature over Attestix's RFC 8785-style canonical body
// against the issuer did:key, plus expiry. Revocation cannot be checked offline.
```

### On-chain anchor (Base L2)

There is no `anchor` CLI command. Use the `anchor_audit_batch` MCP tool or `BlockchainService().anchor_audit_batch(agent_id=...)` in Python. Anchoring requires `pip install "attestix[blockchain]"` and `EVM_PRIVATE_KEY`; it uses Base Sepolia by default and Base mainnet with `BASE_NETWORK=mainnet`. See the [Base L2 anchor walkthrough](/docs/guides/base-l2-anchor).
